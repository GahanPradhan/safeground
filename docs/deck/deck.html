The problem

The right move depends on the hazard and the exact spot — generic advice fails people

  • Disasters hit specific places: Paiporta’s flash flood (2024), the Beirut port explosion (2020), the Antakya earthquake (2023), the Lahaina wildfire (2023).
  • Planners can’t see it in one place: which hazards are on record, which blocks lack a refuge, a hospital or a shelter, and who needs help first.
  • A chatbot alone guesses: in our test, Gemini without computed facts gave the wrong danger level in 14 of 20 cases.

The solution

One platform, three steps — for any place on Earth

1 · Overview

What hits this place?

  • Hazard profile from the place’s own records: floods, rain extremes, earthquakes, cyclones, fires, checked news.
  • Exposure: residents, low ground, schools, elderly homes, hospitals, shelters, open areas.
  • Country emergency numbers.
2 · Readiness

How ready is it?

  • An AI investigator picks the checks that matter here.
  • Readiness before / during / after for people, animals and nature.
  • Ranked, map-linked actions; every number traced to a dataset.
3 · Simulate

If it happens here, what now?

  • 9 hazards, conditions you set, a spot and a person’s situation.
  • Danger zone from official guidance → safe move → nearest safe place → route + Google Maps directions.
  • Plain-English plan; every step cites official advice.

For: city and district disaster teams · companies protecting sites and staff · schools and hospitals planning drills · NGOs pre-positioning relief · residents.

A planning and training tool: no alerts are sent, no personal data is collected, and nothing replaces official instructions.

Accuracy & trust

Every answer is checked by code before anyone sees it

Validator catches invention

In one readiness report the writer’s first two drafts added up residents on their own. Both were rejected as numbers not in any tool result; the third draft passed.

Sources are opened, not trusted

Each news link is fetched and must mention the place and the year. Sites that block bots are checked on their Internet Archive copy. Unchecked links never reach a report.

Unsafe moves are blocked

Basement in a gas leak, downwind in a wildfire, the shore in a tsunami, a doorway in an earthquake, wading through flood water — rejected by rule.

Labelled estimates

Flood, surge and tsunami tiers, gas and fire zones and population are shown with their method and limits.

Unknown stays unknown

Hospital capacity, basements, distance from the coast and whether a building is open are shown as gaps.

No guessed numbers

Emergency numbers come from a checked table per country; if a country is missing, the app says so.

Limits & next steps

What it is not — yet

Limitations

  • Flood, surge and tsunami tiers are estimates from a 30 m elevation model; distance from the coast is not measured.
  • ERG distances are made for transport incidents; the wildfire spread is a rule of thumb, not a fire model.
  • OpenStreetMap gaps: hospital capacity, basements, elderly homes, animals.
  • Each place covers a 3 km circle; conditions (wind, rain, heat) are set by the user.
  • Official advice is from US and Indian sources; local guidance may differ.

Next

  • Live conditions and alerts (Google Weather API, Flood Hub, GDACS) to keep plans current.
  • Local official guidance and languages per country; voice for people who can’t read the screen.
  • Area impact briefs with actions by role (residents, responders, city) and drill exports.
  • Team workspaces and saved scenarios for organisations.
  • Human review of readiness reports with local disaster-management officers.
